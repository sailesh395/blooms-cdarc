Early Intervention

7 early signs your child may benefit from therapy

A gentle guide for parents · 5 min read

As a parent, you know your child best. If something feels like it isn't quite following the usual path, it's worth paying attention — not to worry, but to understand. Early support, when it's needed, can make a remarkable difference. Here are seven signs that a developmental assessment might help.

1. Speech and language are slow to develop

Every child talks in their own time, but there are broad guides. If your child isn't babbling by around 12 months, isn't using single words by 18 months, or isn't combining two words by age two, a speech-and-language assessment can help you understand why and how to support them.

2. Difficulty with everyday physical skills

Trouble with crawling, walking, running, climbing stairs, or with fine-motor tasks like holding a spoon, stacking blocks or using crayons, can be a sign that occupational therapy or physiotherapy would help build strength and coordination.

3. Challenges with social interaction

Limited eye contact, not responding to their name, difficulty playing alongside other children, or not pointing to share interest, are worth discussing with a specialist — especially if you notice several of these together.

4. Strong reactions to sights, sounds or textures

Some children are overwhelmed by loud sounds, bright lights, certain clothing textures or food textures, while others seek out intense sensory input. This can point to sensory processing needs, which respond well to sensory-integration therapy.

5. Difficulty focusing or sitting still

All young children are active, but persistent difficulty with attention, following simple instructions, or sitting for age-appropriate activities may be worth assessing — particularly as your child approaches school age.

6. Losing skills they once had

If your child stops doing something they used to do — words they've dropped, or skills that have faded — it's important to seek advice sooner rather than later.

7. Your instincts tell you something

Never underestimate a parent's intuition. If you have a quiet feeling that your child needs a little extra help, trust it. An assessment can only ever bring clarity and peace of mind.

Why early intervention matters

A young child's brain is remarkably adaptable. The earlier we understand a child's needs and begin the right support, the more we can help them build skills, confidence and independence. Early intervention isn't about labels — it's about opening doors.
